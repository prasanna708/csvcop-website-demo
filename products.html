What each module covers

Two open doors on every lab PC. One module closes each.

Locking Windows down keeps the workstation in order. The network is a separate door: a locked-down PC can still reach every machine on its network, and any program on it can still reach the internet.

Without 21 CFR Part 11 Controls

The workstation is open

  • A setting changed by hand stays changed until the next audit finds it
  • Results can be copied to a USB stick, deleted or renamed
  • Group Policy hides a menu but cannot stop a key, a toolbar button, a drag or the clipboard
  • Nothing records who changed what, or who tried
Closed by 21 CFR Part 11 Controls
Without Micro-segmentation

The network is open

  • Every PC, share, printer and server on the lab network is in reach
  • Any program on the PC, known or not, can reach the internet
  • VLANs and network firewalls see addresses, not the program behind them
  • Firewall rules set by hand get switched off or widened, and nobody notices
Closed by Micro-segmentation

Module 1 · in every licence

21 CFR Part 11 Controls

The Windows side of the workstation: the controls your SOP asks for, held in place by a service, with the routes Windows policy cannot reach closed by an agent in every session — and a record of all of it.

97 numbered controls

Passwords, lockout, Start menu, Run, command prompt, drives, network settings, time and shutdown — each in plain words, side effects included.

Self-healing in 30 seconds

A SYSTEM service compares every control with the encrypted master and puts back what differs, as a logged tamper event.

Session guard

Copy, cut, paste, delete, rename, drag and the clipboard refused at the keys, the toolbar and the file system.

USB by device

Deny everything, deny writing, or allow the exact devices your instruments need, by hardware identity.

Administrators held to account

CSVCOP's own accounts: password policy, rate-limited sign-in, a 20-minute idle sign-out, the password asked again to apply.

Evidence for the auditor

Protected logs, tamper events mirrored to the Windows Event Log, and seven reports a click from PDF.

Module 2 · for every workstation, or the ones you choose

Micro-segmentation

The network side of the workstation: which programs may talk to which machines, enforced by the firewall already built into Windows. No driver, no appliance, no change to the switch — and every refusal on record.

Learning mode first

Before anything is restricted, every connection the PC makes is recorded: program, address, port and protocol. One click makes a line a rule.

Isolate from the local network

The PC stops talking to the other PCs, shares, printers and servers on its network, except what a rule allows. Incoming can be refused too.

Internet for named programs only

Only the programs you name reach the internet. An unknown program, or a known one under a new name, is refused the moment it tries.

Rules in plain words

Allow or block by program or Windows service, address, port and protocol, with presets for a file share, SQL Server, a printer or Remote Desktop, and schedules.

Five minutes to confirm

Before a change, CSVCOP lists what will stop working. A tightening change undoes itself unless it is confirmed within five minutes.

Held in place, on record

The watchdog puts back rules changed outside CSVCOP within 30 seconds, and every refused connection is written down.
